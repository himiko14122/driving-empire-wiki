## What Counts as a Driving Empire Exclusive Event Car

An exclusive event car in Driving Empire is any vehicle that cannot be purchased from the standard dealership at any price. These cars are gated behind time-limited events, anniversary brand collaborations, code redemptions, or hidden map pickups. Unlike the regular inventory that Voldex cycles through with each patch, event exclusives often have a strict window, and once the event ends, the car either disappears entirely or gets vaulted for an unspecified future return.

### Three Tiers of Event Exclusivity

Collectors generally divide Driving Empire rare vehicle drops into three exclusivity tiers based on how the car was originally distributed and how likely it is to return, with the cutoff points determined by the in-game redemption window rather than by the vehicle's horsepower or rarity tag. **Code-Locked** cars like the 2MLIKES Nissan GT-R depend on Voldex reactivating a still-public code, **Seasonal Event** cars such as the 2020 Halloween Ghost lock behind a one-week holiday vendor, and **Build-A-Car** rewards like the Porsche 911 Turbo 50th require players to physically pick up scattered parts before the map-wide despawn timer expires.

| Tier | Distribution Method | Return Likelihood | Examples |
|------|---------------------|-------------------|----------|
| **Code-Locked** | Redeemable through active codes | High during promo windows, then dead | 2MLIKES Nissan GT-R, NASCAR100M Trophies |
| **Seasonal Event** | Halloween, holiday, anniversary collabs | Low — usually once a year | 2020 Halloween Ghost, Anniversary Porsche 911 |
| **Build-A-Car** | Collect parts scattered across the map | Very low — single weekend windows | Porsche 911 Turbo 50th Anniversary |

Code-locked cars technically reappear whenever Voldex reactivates a code during a new promo, which is why SPOOKY, MARCH2026, and 2MLIKES still surface during major updates. Seasonal event cars and Build-A-Car rewards are far more punishing — when the timer hits zero, the parts despawn, the NPC vendor closes, and the only way to obtain the vehicle is through the official Trading system with a player who already owns it.

### Why Voldex Runs Event-Limited Drops

Limited drops serve a dual purpose. They reward dedicated players who log in daily during a specific window, and they create artificial scarcity that drives the in-game Trading economy. According to community data reported by players on the Driving Empire subreddit, anniversary brand collabs like the Porsche 911 Turbo 50th Anniversary can shift the value of a single part to several million in-game dollars on the Trading hub. That kind of economy keeps both collectors and casual players engaged between major content patches.

For a broader look at how every car in the game ranks against each other, the [Driving Empire tier list](/en/tier-list/driving-empire-tier-list/) sorts the full 200+ vehicle roster into S/A/B/C brackets based on drag and circuit performance. Event cars frequently land in S tier because they often carry tuned default stats to celebrate a brand partnership.

## Build-A-Car Anniversary Events and Brand Collabs

Build-A-Car events are the rarest category of Driving Empire exclusive event cars, and they almost always tie into a real-world brand collaboration. Voldex partners with manufacturers like Porsche, BMW, and Brunswick Corporation, then drops a limited pool of unique parts across the map. Players have a short window (usually 48-72 hours) to find every part, assemble the car at a designated NPC, and add the finished vehicle to their garage.

### Porsche 911 Turbo 50th Anniversary

The Porsche 911 Turbo 50th Anniversary event is the most famous Build-A-Car collaboration in Driving Empire history. To celebrate the 50th anniversary of the actual Porsche 911 Turbo, Voldex scattered anniversary-specific parts — body panels, rims, spoilers, badges — across the entire Driving Empire map. Players had to fly, drive, and even boat between hotspots to collect every piece.

| Part Category | Approximate Drop Locations | Rarity Hint |

Because the 50YEARS911TURBO code is now expired, the only current way to get this car is through trading with a player who completed the original event. Community testing shows the finished Porsche 911 Turbo 50th Anniversary sits comfortably in A tier for street circuit racing because of its balanced grip-to-power ratio, which is impressive considering it was a free unlock during the event window.

The Brunswick Big Splash update, which launched in March 2026, marked the first time Voldex partnered with a marine manufacturer. ## Seasonal Exclusive Cars: Halloween, Holidays, and Anniversaries

Seasonal events are the most recurring source of Driving Empire seasonal exclusive cars. Voldex runs a Halloween drop almost every October, plus winter holiday content in December, and an anniversary celebration tied to the game's launch date in February.

### Halloween Drops

The Halloween drop is the longest-running seasonal tradition in Driving Empire. Every October, Voldex decorates the map with pumpkins, ghost decorations, and a limited-time NPC vendor who sells (or trades) exclusive cars for Halloween currency. Past Halloween exclusives have included a 2020 Ghost-themed coupe and a series of custom-wrapped muscle cars with black-and-orange liveries.

|----------------|--------------|---------------|----------------|

| **2025** | Trick-or-Treat Truck | Code SPOOKY during October | Code expired |

The SPOOKY code, which historically granted 80,000 in-game cash and acted as a Halloween gateway, is one of the longest-running Driving Empire exclusive event car promo codes. When SPOOKY reactivates during October, it's usually accompanied by a fresh Halloween car drop, so logging in during the first week of October is critical for collectors.

### Winter and Anniversary Drops

Winter holiday events usually add a snow-wrapped variant of an existing car plus a new vehicle tied to the theme. Anniversary events in February are more ambitious because they celebrate the game itself, and they tend to feature brand collabs or major new modes. The 50YEARS911TURBO code that shipped with the Porsche 911 Turbo 50th Anniversary is the clearest example of an anniversary reward.

The driving empire car collection guide approach to seasonal cars is simple: log in every day during the event window, check the newsfeed on the official Roblox page, and redeem every active code immediately because most of them expire within 30-90 days. The MARCH2026 code, for example, rewarded a 2012 Chevrolet Camaro ZL1 and was tied to the Big Splash update, but it has already expired as of the most recent patch notes.

## Trading, Rerolls, and the Event Car Economy

Once an event ends, the only way to obtain a vaulted Driving Empire exclusive event car is through the official Trading hub. Trading is player-to-player, and the value of each car fluctuates based on rarity, demand, and whether Voldex has hinted at a rerun.

### How to Value a Vaulted Event Car

There is no official price list for traded cars because Voldex deliberately keeps the market organic, which means trade values in Driving Empire fluctuate based on player demand rather than fixed supply metrics. The community has settled on three rough benchmarks—original event cost, current meta use, and rerun rumors—to anchor negotiations, and these same benchmarks explain why long-vaulted Halloween exclusives like the 2020 Ghost Coupe or 50th-anniversary Porsche 911 Turbo Build-A-Car command multi-A-tier trade value today.

| Benchmark | How It's Used | Example |
|-----------|---------------|---------|
| **Original Event Cost** | Baseline value of how hard it was to obtain | Porsche 911 Turbo 50th (Build-A-Car) — very high baseline |
| **Current Meta Use** | Demand based on drag/circuit performance | Haunted Sport Sedan — mid demand |
| **Rerun Rumors** | Speculative value if Voldex teases a return | Anniversary cars — high speculative demand |

A common strategy among veteran collectors is to hold event cars in their garage rather than trade them away during the first year, because the value tends to appreciate as the rerun window slips. The 2020 Ghost Coupe, for example, is now worth multiple A-tier standard cars in trade value because Voldex has not rerun it since the original Halloween event.

### Avoiding Trade Scams

Trade scams are the single biggest risk in the Driving Empire Trading hub, and they spike during major events when newer players try to get an exclusive car without understanding the value. The most common scam pattern is offering a low-tier standard car plus Robux for a high-tier event exclusive, then never delivering the Robux after the trade completes. The safest countermeasure is to trade only through the official Trading menu (never direct garage transfers) and to require both sides to confirm a Robux component in a separate transaction that the victim can reverse if needed.

Collectors who want to stay ahead of upcoming drops should also follow the [Driving Empire upcoming cars leak](/en/vehicles/driving-empire-upcoming-cars-leak/) coverage, because leaks sometimes surface days before Voldex officially announces a new event, and early preparation is the difference between completing a Build-A-Car and missing it entirely.
