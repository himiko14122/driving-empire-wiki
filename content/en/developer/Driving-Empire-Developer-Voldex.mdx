## Origins: Alex Singer and the Pre-Voldex Build

Driving Empire began life well before Voldex entered the picture. The original build was created by Alex Singer under the studio name **Nocturne Entertainment**, and it launched on Roblox in late 2018 as a stripped-down driving test that focused purely on vehicle handling rather than the licensed showroom it eventually became. Players who joined during the alpha period remember a much smaller map, a handful of unlicensed sports cars, and drag racing as the only real event type.

### What Nocturne Entertainment Built First

Nocturne Entertainment's first publicly listed Roblox experience, before Driving Empire carried the studio's name, was a smaller test place used by Alex Singer to prototype vehicle physics. Those early prototypes directly informed the gear-driven handling model that survived into the modern game, which is why long-time players still describe the core driving feel as "arcade-with-weight" rather than a pure simulator.

The original **Driving Empire Nocturne Entertainment** release used placeholder car models and a single drag strip, but it introduced the cash loop, dealership UI, and dealership-to-garage ownership flow that the current version still uses. The progression skeleton, earn cash, buy a car, trade up to a better car, was set in those first months, and the **Driving Empire Alex Singer Voldex** handoff later preserved that loop instead of replacing it.

The handoff from Nocturne Entertainment to Voldex was not a hostile takeover or a public studio merger. Instead, the **Driving Empire studio acquisition** happened quietly as Alex Singer moved into a different role inside the project, and Voldex gradually took over publishing, live-ops, and brand relations. The result is a game whose DNA is still Alex Singer's original Roblox driving test, but whose day-to-day operation now sits inside a multi-game studio.

### Why the Name Change Mattered

For players, the transition from **Nocturne Entertainment** to Voldex was visible in three places: the developer credit on the Roblox game page, the Discord server branding, and the size of the update patch notes.
