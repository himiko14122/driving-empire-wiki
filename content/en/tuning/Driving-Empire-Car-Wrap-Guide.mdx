A Driving Empire car wrap guide is the single fastest way to make your ride stand out at the drag strip, on street circuits, and in car meets around the map. Customization in this Voldex-developed Roblox driving sim goes far beyond picking a single color — wraps, body kits, wheels, spoilers, and neon underglow all stack to create a unique look, and each layer is tied to its own in-game currency and unlock path. If you want your car to look as fast as it drives, mastering every layer of the visual tuner is just as important as learning the race lines described in our [Driving Empire beginner guide](/en/guides/driving-empire-beginner-guide/).

## Getting Into the Customization Menu

Every licensed vehicle in Driving Empire — from entry-level tuners to top-tier Lamborghinis and Porsches — opens the same in-garage customization interface when you spawn it. The customization menu is split into a handful of visual tabs, each controlling a single aspect of the car: Wrap & Paint, Body Kit, Wheels, Spoiler, Underglow, and a few smaller accessory slots. Players access the garage by pulling up the side menu while standing next to a vehicle they own, and confirmed changes save instantly to the car.

According to the [official Driving Empire Roblox page](https://www.roblox.com/games/3351674303/Driving-Empire), there are more than 200 licensed vehicles spread across drag, street, ATV, and boating modes, and each car shares the same customizer with the same tab layout. That consistency means once you learn the wrap system on a starter car, you can apply the same logic to a 2012 Chevrolet Camaro ZL1 unlocked through the MARCH2026 code or a 2017 Nissan GT-R unlocked via 2MLIKES.

### What You Need Before Customizing

| Requirement | What It Does | How to Get It |
|---|---|---|
| **Customization Kit** | Unlocks the visual customizer on a car you already own | Code **10KITS** grants 10, **CUSTOMIZATION2025** grants 10 |
| **Cash** | Standard currency for paint colors, basic wraps, and most body parts | Earn through drag races, street circuits, and free-roam drifting |
| **Trophies** | Premium currency for premium wraps, rare spoilers, and exclusive neon shades | Win tournaments, complete limited-time Build-A-Car events |
| **License Rank** | Some body kit and spoiler options unlock only at higher tiers | Progress through Street, Drag, and Circuit license tiers |

Players regularly confirm that a single 10KITS or CUSTOMIZATION2025 code is enough to fully customize the cheapest starter car, so unlocking the customizer is the cheapest visual upgrade you can make. Cash is what you spend most on, while Trophies unlock the premium wrap palettes and the rarer underglow hues.

## Wrap and Paint Layer in the Driving Empire Car Wrap Guide

The wrap tab is the foundation of any custom build, and it controls two related but distinct features: a base paint color and an optional wrap pattern on top. According to community testing, the wrap layer sits above the base coat, so the base color still shows through gaps in the pattern (such as the windows or panel edges that the wrap does not cover).

### Driving Empire Paint Colors List

The base coat palette covers standard automotive colors plus a handful of matte, metallic, and pearlescent variants, and each finish is essentially the "canvas" your wrap or body kit gets layered onto. In Driving Empire, paint type directly affects how neon underglow and chrome trims read on screen — gloss amplifies reflections, while matte absorbs them — so the color picker choice should align with your planned wrap pattern, wheel options, and spoiler profile before you commit any Robux to the re-spray. Skipping that step is the most common reason a wrap ends up looking muddy against a base it wasn't tuned for.

| Finish Type | Visual Effect | Best Paired With |
|---|---|---|
| **Gloss** | High-shine, reflective clear coat | Neon underglow, chrome wheels |
| **Matte** | Flat, non-reflective surface | Bright wraps, aggressive body kits |
| **Metallic** | Subtle flake, catches light at angles | Classic muscle builds |
| **Pearlescent** | Color shifts under sunlight | Premium supercar builds |

Among the most-used base colors reported by players are **Pearl White**, **Midnight Black**, **Racing Red**, **Gunmetal Gray**, and **Nardo Gray**. Nardo Gray in particular has become a community favorite because it pairs with almost any wrap pattern without clashing.

### Wrap Pattern Options

Wrap patterns are decal-style designs that overlay the base paint in the Driving Empire customizer, sitting on top of the original coat rather than replacing it. Players have logged a wide range of patterns, from minimalist two-tone splits to pattern-heavy tribal decals, each one confirmed in the in-game catalog and tested across body kits like the roadster, sedan, and SUV chassis. Because the base coat still peeks through at panel edges, choosing a complementary contrast color is the key to making these overlay designs actually stand out.

- **Solid block wraps** — single-color covers that essentially act as a second paint layer

- **Two-tone splits** — horizontal or vertical color divisions across the body

- **Racing stripes** — center hood-to-trunk strips with adjustable width

- **Carbon-fiber sections** — applied to the hood, roof, or trunk only

- **Gradient fades** — color transitions from front to rear

- **Tribal and geometric decals** — pattern-heavy designs for show cars

When choosing a wrap pattern, remember the base coat still peeks through small gaps. Pairing a light base with a dark wrap, or a dark base with a light wrap, creates a layered look that a single coat cannot replicate.

## Driving Empire Body Kit Options

Body kits are the most expensive visual layer for most cars, because they replace the front bumper, side skirts, and rear bumper all at once. According to player reports, each car has between three and five body kit presets, and each preset dramatically changes the silhouette of the vehicle.

| Body Kit Style | Visual Character | Suggested Wrap Pairing |
|---|---|---|
| **Stock** | Factory body lines, clean look | Solid gloss paint, no wrap |
| **Street** | Lowered stance, subtle lip on front bumper | Racing stripes on matte or pearlescent base |
| **Widebody** | Aggressive fender flares, vented hood | Two-tone split wrap with carbon-fiber hood |
| **Track** | Large rear diffuser, front splitter | Single solid wrap, neon underglow |
| **Show** | Chrome accents, smooth bumpers | Pearl white base with gradient fade wrap |

Widebody kits are the most popular in drag builds because the visual mass of the flared fenders matches the muscular look of high-horsepower cars. Track kits are more common on circuit builds where the diffuser and splitter pair with the race-focused aero that the street circuit mode rewards.

Body kits do not affect performance — they are purely cosmetic. This means you can safely swap between a widebody and a track kit without changing how the car drives, so players tend to pick based on aesthetic preference rather than racing advantage. For reference, the racing side of car selection is covered in detail in the [Driving Empire tier list](/en/tier-list/driving-empire-tier-list/), which sorts vehicles by drag, circuit, and ATV performance.

## Driving Empire Wheel Options

Wheels are the second-most visible upgrade, and they often determine whether a build reads as a street tuner, a track car, or a luxury cruiser. The customizer offers several wheel categories, and within each category there are multiple sizes and finishes.

| Wheel Category | Visual Style | Build Match |
|---|---|---|
| **Stock** | Factory alloys, modest diameter | Clean OEM+ look |
| **Sport** | Five-spoke or split-spoke, mid diameter | Daily-driver street build |
| **Tuner** | Deep dish, aggressive offset, large diameter | JDM-style widebody drag build |
| **Track** | Lightweight Y-spoke or mesh, small diameter | Circuit-focused builds |
| **Luxury** | Multi-spoke chrome, polished finish | Show cars and cruisers |
| **Off-Road** | Beadlock-style with chunky sidewall | ATV off-road mode builds |

Wheel finishes are equally varied. Players report finishes including **Chrome**, **Gloss Black**, **Matte Black**, **Bronze**, **Gold**, **Gunmetal**, and **Body-Color Matched**. Chrome wheels against a dark wrap is a long-time community favorite because the reflection contrast reads clearly even at distance, which is useful at car meets where the camera is rarely close.

Wheel size also matters visually: oversized wheels look aggressive but can clip the widebody fender flares if the offset is too aggressive. Players who run widebody kits typically stick to mid-range wheel diameters to avoid the tires poking past the fender line.

## Driving Empire Spoiler Options

Spoilers are the smallest visual layer but often the most photographed, because they sit at eye level behind the car and visually cap the trunk line. Every spoiler in the customizer is rear-mounted, and most vehicles support several heights and styles — including the lip, pedestal, GT wing, drag wing, and ducktail profiles detailed in the table below.

| Spoiler Style | Visual Profile | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| **Lip** | Subtle trunk-edge lip | Stock look, OEM+ builds |
| **Pedestal** | Small wing on two uprights | Sport sedans, JDM tuners |
| **GT Wing** | Wide flat wing, low or high mount | Track and widebody builds |
| **Drag Wing** | Tall uprights, narrow blade | Straight-line drag builds |
| **Ducktail** | Integrated trunk lip, vintage profile | Classic muscle cars |
| **No Spoiler** | Clean trunk lid | Show builds, sleeper looks |

A drag wing against a widebody kit is the visual signature of the drag racing scene in Driving Empire, while a low-mounted GT wing is what most players pick for circuit builds. The ducktail suits classic muscle cars like the 2012 Chevrolet Camaro ZL1 and other Chevrolet models, and the lip spoiler is the default choice for builds aiming at an OEM+ look.

## Driving Empire Neon Underglow Guide

Underglow is the layer that most dramatically changes how a car looks at night, because the light source sits below the chassis and reflects off the road surface. According to the customization menu, underglow controls three independent variables: color, brightness, and zone.

| Underglow Zone | What It Lights |
|---|---|
| **Front** | Under the front bumper and chin spoiler |
| **Side** | Below the side skirts, both left and right |
| **Rear** | Under the rear bumper and diffuser |

### Underglow Color Options

Players have documented roughly twenty distinct underglow palettes in the customizer menu, spanning four loose families — standard primary RGB, premium jewel tones, white temperature variants, and the trophy-gated color-shift preset — and the exact selection can vary slightly between mobile and PC clients. The color picker also remembers the last shade used on a per-vehicle basis, so swapping between a tuned street build and a neon-heavy show car does not force you to re-select from scratch every session. Because the glow renders independently of your chosen paint colors list, you can pair the same wrap setup with very different underglow choices to create visually distinct variants of the same body kit without buying extra parts.

- **Standard RGB** — Red, Green, Blue, Yellow, Orange, Purple, Pink, Cyan

- **Premium shades** — Ice Blue, Hot Pink, Lime Green, Deep Purple, Sunset Orange

- **White tones** — Pure White, Warm White, Cool White

- **Color-shift** — Multi-color cycling pattern (Trophy-unlocked)

The premium and color-shift shades are gated behind Trophies, which is why most free-to-play players stick to standard RGB. Ice Blue is a particular favorite for circuit builds, while Hot Pink and Lime Green are the most common picks for show cars at the indoor car meet locations.

### Brightness and Syncing

Underglow brightness scales from a subtle glow to a near-daylight level, and the brightness can be set per zone or synced across all three zones. Synced bright underglow reads as aggressive and works well for show builds, while asymmetric underglow (front dim, rear bright) is a community favorite for drag builds because the rear glow matches the look of nitrous purging at the line.

## Build Combos That Always Read Well

Combining the layers well matters more than any single choice, because Driving Empire's wrap, body kit, wheels, spoiler, and neon underglow each occupy a distinct visual zone of the car model and compete for attention when stacked. The following combos were reported by the community to read cleanly at distance, in screenshots, and at car meets, balancing finish, color temperature, and silhouette rather than stacking the rarest item in every slot.

| Build Theme | Wrap | Body Kit | Wheels | Spoiler | Underglow |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **JDM Drag** | Black base, red tribal wrap | Widebody | Bronze tuner, deep dish | Drag wing | Red, synced |
| **Track Day** | White base, no wrap | Track | Black mesh, small diameter | GT wing, low mount | Ice Blue, medium |
| **Show Car** | Pearl white base, gradient fade wrap | Show | Chrome luxury | Lip or ducktail | Warm White, subtle |
| **Street Sleeper** | Matte gray base, no wrap | Stock | Stock alloys | No spoiler | None |
| **Muscle Classic** | Racing red base, twin black stripes | Street | Gunmetal sport | Ducktail | None |

When choosing a combo, balance is more important than picking the most expensive option in every slot. A widebody kit with chrome luxury wheels and a drag wing, for example, will look mismatched because every layer is fighting the others. Pick one anchor layer — the layer that defines the build theme — and let the other layers support it.

## Tuning for the Map, Not Just the Camera

Customization is purely visual, so it does not change drag times, circuit lap speed, or ATV handling. However, players do report that lighter-looking builds (white bases, no spoiler, no underglow) are easier to spot from far away on the drag strip start line, which makes judging the Christmas tree slightly easier for new players. Conversely, aggressive widebody builds with bright underglow are easier to see in your peripheral vision at the rear of street circuit packs, which is useful when defending position.

For drivers more interested in raw speed than visual style, the [Driving Empire fastest car](/en/tier-list/driving-empire-fastest-car/) ranking is the better starting point. Pure drag cars and pure circuit cars do not always look the same as the most popular visual builds, so it helps to know which cars top the performance charts before committing Trophies to a wrap pattern that you might want to swap later.

## How to Save and Share Your Build

Every customization change saves instantly to the car, and the build is stored on the vehicle, not on the player. This means switching cars resets your visual setup, so players who own multiple cars typically build a theme per car rather than re-customizing each time they switch.

To share a build visually, players use the in-game photo mode, which captures the car in a controlled environment with all customizations applied. Photo mode is also where most community guides source their reference screenshots, so the look of any wrap or kit you see in a community showcase is exactly what the customizer produces in your own garage once you spend the same currency on the same options.

## Final Notes on the Driving Empire Car Wrap Guide

A complete Driving Empire car wrap guide covers all five visual layers — wrap, body kit, wheels, spoiler, and underglow — because each one is independent and stackable. The wrap layer sets the color identity, the body kit sets the silhouette, the wheels set the stance, the spoiler sets the profile, and the underglow sets the night-time personality. Mastering all five is what separates a generic starter car from a recognizable show build, and the entire customizer unlocks with a single 10KITS code on a starter car you already own.

The customizer is also the cheapest way to refresh a long-term car without spending real money. Premium shades and color-shift underglow are Trophy-gated, but every other layer is reachable with cash earned in normal races, which makes visual progression a parallel track to license rank progression.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I unlock the customizer on a new car in Driving Empire?**

You need a **Customization Kit** for each car you want to tune. The codes **10KITS** and **CUSTOMIZATION2025** each give 10 kits, and you enter them through the gear icon in the in-game settings menu. Once applied, the customizer tab opens permanently for that vehicle.

**What is the best wrap combo for drag racing builds?**

The community favorite for drag builds is a **Midnight Black** base with a **Racing Red** racing-stripe wrap, paired with a **Widebody** body kit, **Bronze Tuner** wheels, and a **Drag Wing** spoiler. The contrast reads clearly from the staging lanes and matches the aggressive drag look.

**Are body kits and spoilers only cosmetic?**

Yes. According to player testing, body kits, wheels, spoilers, underglow, and wraps are all purely visual and do not change drag times, top speed, handling, or braking. Performance is determined by the car's base stats, which are covered in vehicle-tier rankings rather than in the customizer.

**Can I sync underglow across all zones?**

Yes. The underglow menu includes a **Sync** toggle that applies the same color and brightness to the front, side, and rear zones at once. You can also set each zone independently if you want an asymmetric look, which is a popular choice for drag builds that use brighter rear underglow than front.

**How do I get Trophy-only wraps and premium underglow colors?**

Trophies are earned by winning tournaments, completing limited-time **Build-A-Car** events, and progressing through license ranks. Premium wrap palettes and the **Color-Shift** underglow mode are gated behind Trophy spending, so saving Trophies for these rare options is more efficient than spending them on cash-only items you could afford with race earnings.
