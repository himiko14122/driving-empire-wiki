## Common 30-Day Pitfalls and How to Dodge Them

Most new players fail not because of bad cars but because of cash leaks. Resale value is the silent killer. Selling a tuned car returns roughly 60% of the purchase price plus any tuning fees, and beginner players who churn vehicles every two days lose 40% of their net worth to depreciation. Hold each car for at least 10 days, finish the Build-A-Car event tied to its brand, and only resell when a newer model enters the meta.

Another frequent mistake is ignoring the maintenance and repair timers. Cars that crash or run 50+ races without service lose grip and braking response, which silently drops your win rate. Community data shows that tuned-and-serviced cars win 73% of their street circuit heats, while tuned-but-unserviced cars drop to 51%. Service every 30 races.

Finally, do not sleep on boating. The Brunswick water physics update added genuine depth to the meta, and the 420 Outrage currently sits in the top three boating payout vehicles. Players who ignore water racing are leaving roughly 25% of total available cash on the table per week, and that gap compounds over 30 days into a hypercar tier difference.

No, Driving Empire requires an active session for any race payout, including drag strip.
