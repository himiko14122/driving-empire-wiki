## What Build-A-Car Events Actually Drop in Driving Empire

Build-A-Car events are time-limited scavenger hunts where Voldex scatters branded parts across the open map. Collecting every required part automatically assembles the car in your inventory, so you skip the dealership price tag and the limited-stock rotation. These events typically run alongside a real-world anniversary or manufacturer partnership, which is why the rewards tend to be licensed vehicles rather than generic tuner models.

### Event Structure and Reward Logic

The format has stayed consistent across recent events: parts spawn at fixed map coordinates, the timer runs 7-14 days, and the assembled car stays in your garage permanently once you turn in the final part. If you miss the window, the parts despawn and the car returns to the dealership at a heavily inflated price, or in some cases simply disappears from the catalog.

The Porsche 911 Turbo 50th Anniversary was one of the first Build-A-Car drops, commemorating the real-world 1974 launch of the 930 Turbo. According to the [Driving Empire official Roblox page](https://www.roblox.com/games/3351674303/Driving-Empire), the game ships with 200+ licensed vehicles, and event-only cars make up a meaningful slice of the collector tier. Community testing on the Chinese Smzdm guide thread confirms the 50th Anniversary drop used a 5-part scavenger hunt with all parts confined to the main land map, not the boating zones added in the Big Splash update.

### Why These Cars Are Worth Chasing

The Porsche 911 Turbo 50th Anniversary sits comfortably in the top S-tier for street circuits thanks to its cornering grip and braking stability, and it remains competitive in drag races when tuned correctly. Most players run it as a daily driver because it handles every race mode without a clear weakness. The Nissan GT-R and Camaro ZL1, both reachable through the active code system, fill similar roles but trade slightly different stat profiles, which is why the code routes below pair well with the Build-A-Car grind.

## Driving Empire Porsche 911 Turbo 50th Anniversary Parts Location Map

The Porsche event scattered five parts across the land map, and each one has a known landmark anchor so you can route them in a single lap. The community-documented spawn points below are based on Smzdm player walkthroughs cross-referenced with multiple YouTube guides; treat coordinates as approximate and confirm visually when you arrive.

### Part 1 — Dealer Showroom Roof

The first part sits on the roof of the main car dealership in the dealership plaza. Spawn in, run straight north through the showroom floor, climb the rear stairwell, and the part glows on the roof edge facing the drag strip. This is the easiest pickup and a natural warm-up before the longer routes.

### Part 2 — Drag Strip Tower

Head to the drag strip and climb the timing tower on the south side. The part is tucked behind the announcer booth, which means you need to jump the railing rather than take the stairs. Players who hit the strip for tuning test runs can grab this part in under a minute between runs.

### Part 3 — Off-Road Trailhead Sign

Drive an ATV or SUV to the off-road trailhead and look for the wooden signpost at the entrance to the dirt loop. The part spawns at the base of the sign, partially clipped into the ground texture. An off-road vehicle is required because the trailhead sits beyond the normal road grid.

### Part 4 — Airport Hangar Back Wall

The fourth part requires a plane ticket from the airport terminal. Once you land, walk to the back wall of the largest hangar and the part is sitting on a crate near the fuel drums. This is the part that trips up new players because the airport was added later and is easy to miss on the standard map.

### Part 5 — Mountain Overlook

The final part is the trickiest. Drive a high-grip car up the winding mountain road, then take the footpath to the overlook cliff edge. The part rests on the stone railing facing the city, and you need to jump the gap from the parking area to reach it. Bring the Porsche itself if you have already assembled it, because the route doubles as a handling test for the reward car.

### Tuning the ZL1 for Drag Strip

The ZL1 ships with a balanced street tune that favors grip over raw power, which is the wrong setup for drag racing. Swap the tire compound to drag radials, raise the final-drive ratio for better launch RPM, and shift the gear ratios toward shorter throws. ## Farming Routes That Keep the Build-A-Car Grind Moving

Even with every part location mapped, the Build-A-Car event demands cash for fuel, plane tickets, and the occasional repair. A solid farming loop turns the downtime between part pickups into progress rather than waiting in spawn. The routes below assume you are running the Porsche 911 Turbo 50th Anniversary event and want to keep cash flowing while you hunt parts.

### Tier 1 — Quarter-Mile Farming (Highest Cash per Minute)

The drag strip pays the most consistent cash per minute in the game, and the loop is short enough to keep rematch cooldowns from throttling your income. Spawn at the dealership, drive to the strip, win three races, bank the payout, then run a quick part pickup on the way back to the dealer. The whole cycle takes roughly five minutes and is repeatable indefinitely.

### Tier 2 — Street Circuit Cruise (Balanced Income and Bounty)

Street circuit races pay slightly less per minute than drag runs but offer more experience and a cleaner break from the burnout of quarter-mile sprints. The route works well as a cooldown between drag sessions and gives you a chance to practice the cornering technique you will need for the Porsche handling test on the mountain overlook.

### Tier 3 — ATV Off-Road Trail (Best When Server Is Crowded)

The off-road trail is the lowest-paying of the three primary modes, but it shines when the drag strip is packed with rematch-queuing players. Trail laps are also the natural warm-up for collecting the off-road trailhead part, so you can stack part runs and cash runs in the same trip.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are the Porsche 911 Turbo 50th Anniversary parts still available in 2026?**

The original 50YEARS911TURBO code expired long ago, but Voldex has rerun the Build-A-Car event format with new parts and new locations for every major anniversary cycle. Check the in-game event banner and the official Discord before you start a route, because spawn points shift between reruns. The mechanics — collect five parts, assemble automatically, keep the car — stay the same across reruns.

**Can I get both the Nissan GT-R and the Camaro ZL1 from the same code drop?**

No, each code unlocks a single specific car. The **2MLIKES** code drops the 2017 Nissan GT-R and the **MARCH2026** code drops the 2012 Chevrolet Camaro ZL1, and they are independent redemptions. Stack them in the same session by redeeming both codes back-to-back from the gear-icon Codes menu, which takes about ten seconds per code.

**What is the fastest Build-A-Car part route on a fresh account?**

Start at the dealership roof, run south to the drag strip tower, swap to an ATV and clear the off-road trailhead, then fly to the airport hangar and finish at the mountain overlook in the Porsche itself if you have already assembled it. The full loop takes 15-20 minutes for a player who knows the map and roughly 30-40 minutes for a first-timer who needs to explore each landmark.

**Do expired Driving Empire codes ever come back?**

Some codes return during anniversary or milestone events, but most stay retired permanently. The **50YEARS911TURBO** code, for example, has not been reissued since the original Porsche 50th Anniversary drop. Treat every code as a one-time opportunity and redeem the full active list the moment a new patch ships, because the expiration date is rarely announced in advance.
